## Grade 11 English Language Arts (Level M): Full EngageNY Curriculum & Digital Library Integration

### Executive Overview
Grade 11 English Language Arts (Level M) provides a complete, rigorous, standards-aligned high school curriculum spanning 155 lessons across 4 core modules. Anchored in foundational American rhetoric, Shakespearean dramatic tragedy, the African American intellectual tradition, early feminist philosophy, modern war narratives, and psychological realism, Level M equips junior students with collegiate-level literary analysis, rhetorical evaluation, and research argumentation skills.

All anchor literary selections are directly integrated with the Digital Library as standalone reading pages (`/library/?page=<slug>`), providing distraction-free close reading without cluttering book catalog shelves.

---

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Interactions of Elements, Tragic Flaw, and Gender Autonomy (39 Lessons)
*Theme: How Do Authors Develop Complex Characters, Dramatic Tension, and Central Ideas?*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–6): Dramatic Monologue & Aesthetic Control**
  - Robert Browning: *"My Last Duchess"*
  - *Focus: Ferrara setting, dramatic monologue conventions, speaker arrogance, implied violence, and aesthetic control.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 7–31): Classical Tragedy & Moral Collapse**
  - William Shakespeare: *The Tragedy of Hamlet, Prince of Denmark*
  - *Focus: Soliloquies ("To be or not to be"), feigned madness, dramatic foils (Laertes, Fortinbras), corruption, and existential dread.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 32–39): Literary Autonomy & Social Constraints**
  - Virginia Woolf: *A Room of One's Own* (Chapter 3)
  - *Focus: Material conditions for artistic creation, social constraints on women, and the tragic fate of "Judith Shakespeare."*

#### Module 2: Rhetoric, Identity, and Agency in American Democracy (40 Lessons)
*Theme: Persuasive Rhetoric, Democratic Foundations, and Civil Rights*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–26): Emancipation, Rhetoric & Civil Rights**
  - W.E.B. Du Bois: *"Of Our Spiritual Strivings"* (*The Souls of Black Folk*)
  - Booker T. Washington: *"Atlanta Compromise Speech"*
  - Thomas Jefferson: *The Declaration of Independence*
  - *Focus: "Double-consciousness", the veil, accommodation vs. agitation, and unalienable democratic rights.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 27–40): Women's Agency & Suffrage**
  - Elizabeth Cady Stanton: *"An Address by Elizabeth Cady Stanton"* (Seneca Falls, 1848)
  - Audre Lorde: *"From the House of Yemanjá"*
  - *Focus: Women's suffrage, maternal heritage, racial and gender duality, and poetic imagery.*

#### Module 3: Inquiry, Investigation, and Synthesis (38 Lessons)
*Theme: Researching Complex Questions & Academic Argumentation*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–11): Initiating Inquiry & Gathering Evidence**
  - *Focus: Formulating research questions, inquiry paths, and vetting academic sources.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 12–26): Evaluating Sources & Synthesizing Perspectives**
  - *Focus: Assessing credibility, tracking competing claims, and building multi-perspective research portfolios.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 27–38): Evidence-Based Argument Paper Writing**
  - *Focus: Drafting, peer reviewing, and finalizing formal multi-page research-based argument papers.*

#### Module 4: Narrative Technique, Selfhood, and Social Awakening (38 Lessons)
*Theme: Point of View, Trauma, and Personal Liberation*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–16): Narrative Craft & War Experience**
  - Tim O'Brien: *"On the Rainy River"* (*The Things They Carried*)
  - Louise Erdrich: *"The Red Convertible"*
  - *Focus: First-person point of view, moral cowardice vs. courage, Vietnam War trauma, and brotherhood.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 17–38): Social Awakening & Autonomy**
  - Kate Chopin: *The Awakening*
  - *Focus: Edna Pontellier's emerging selfhood, Creole domestic expectations, the sea motif, and individual freedom.*

### Interactive Features & Tools
- **Digital Library Anchor Links:** Every lesson features a direct link opening the corresponding reading in the Digital Library (`/library/?page=<slug>`).
- **Close Reading Question Sets:** Focused analytical questions examining authorial diction, rhetoric, and syntax.
- **Synthesizing Quick Writes:** Structured writing prompts evaluated against Common Core short-response rubrics.
- **Auto-Saving Response Fields:** Student work is continuously preserved in browser `localStorage`.
- **Teacher Answer Keys:** Worked exemplar solutions with evidence citations and scoring guides.
- **1-Click Printing:** `printProblemSet()` formats clean worksheets for offline binders and classroom submissions.

---

### Accessibility & UDL Features
- **WCAG AA/AAA Compliance:** 100% keyboard navigable (`Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `Enter`, `Space`, `Esc`).
- **High-Contrast Focus Indicators:** Theme-adaptive `:focus-visible` focus rings.
- **Universal Design for Learning:** OpenDyslexic font support, adjustable text sizing, line spacing, Irlen tints, and speech read-aloud tools.
- **Offline Resiliency:** Full curriculum data and reading pages function completely offline via service worker caching.
