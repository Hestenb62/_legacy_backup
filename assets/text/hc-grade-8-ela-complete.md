## Grade 8 English Language Arts (Level J): Full Expeditionary Learning / EngageNY Curriculum

### Executive Overview
Grade 8 English Language Arts (Level J) provides an extensive, inquiry-driven middle school curriculum on Hesten's Learning platform. Spanning 239 comprehensive lessons across 6 full modules (Modules 1, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, and 4), Level J empowers 8th-grade students to investigate complex literary and informational texts, evaluate ethical dilemmas, analyze character motivations, and synthesize evidence into structured arguments.

With the completion of Grade 8, the platform's verified curriculum expands to **2,547 total lessons** across Levels A through N, with 16 dedicated Digital Library reading pages supporting Grade 8 study.

---

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Study of Character and Thematic Development — *Inside Out & Back Again* (40 Lessons)
*Theme: How do critical incidents reveal character and shape universal refugee experiences?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–14): Coming of Age & The Fall of Saigon**
  - Thanhha Lai: *Inside Out & Back Again* ("1975: Year of the Cat", "Papaya Tree")
  - Tod Olson: *"The Vietnam Wars: 1945–1975"*
  - *Focus: Analyzing Ha's character, free-verse poetry, historical context of the Vietnam War, and the fall of Saigon.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–20): Fleeing and Finding Home**
  - Thanhha Lai: *Inside Out & Back Again* ("Saigon is Gone", "The Open Sea", "First Clean Clothes")
  - Arthur Brice: *"Children of War: Stories of Young Refugees"*
  - *Focus: Universal themes of displacement, culture shock, adapting to Alabama, and resilience.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–6): Culminating Free-Verse Poetry Performance Task**
  - *Focus: Researching refugee experiences from various world cultures and composing two free-verse mentor-text narrative poems.*

#### Module 2A: Taking a Stand — *To Kill a Mockingbird* & The Golden Rule (40 Lessons)
*Theme: What does it mean to take a stand for moral principles in the face of societal prejudice?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–19): Speeches on Taking a Stand & Launching the Novel**
  - Shirley Chisholm: *"Equal Rights for Women"* (1969)
  - Sojourner Truth: *"Ain't I a Woman?"* (1851)
  - Lyndon B. Johnson: *"The Great Society"* (1964)
  - Harper Lee: *To Kill a Mockingbird* (Part 1, Scout & Atticus)
  - *Focus: Analyzing rhetoric, central claims, counterclaims, and establishing structured note-taking routines.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–16): Case Study: Atticus Takes a Stand**
  - Harper Lee: *To Kill a Mockingbird* (Part 2, The Trial of Tom Robinson & Mrs. Dubose)
  - Rudyard Kipling: *"If—"*
  - *Focus: Character study of Atticus, moral courage, courtroom drama, and writing an argumentative literary analysis essay.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–5): Readers Theater: Dramatic Montage on the Golden Rule**
  - *Focus: Scripting and performing Readers Theater scenes illustrating empathy and moral integrity.*

#### Module 2B: The Universal Theme of Control — *A Midsummer Night's Dream* (40 Lessons)
*Theme: How do characters attempt to control others, and what are the consequences?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–18): Shakespeare's Craft & Athenian Law**
  - William Shakespeare: *A Midsummer Night's Dream* (Acts 1 & 2)
  - *Focus: The universal appeal of Shakespeare, authorship controversy, Egeus's patriarchal control, and magical manipulation in the enchanted woods.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–18): "What Fools These Mortals Be" & Pyramus and Thisbe**
  - William Shakespeare: *A Midsummer Night's Dream* (Acts 3, 4, 5)
  - Ovid: *"Pyramus and Thisbe"* (Metamorphoses, Book IV)
  - *Focus: Tracing manipulation, Bottom's transformation, structural adaptation of Greek myth, and argument essay on interpersonal control.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–4): Culminating Character Confessional Narrative**
  - *Focus: Writing a first-person confessional narrative explaining a character's attempts at control, motivation, and outcome.*

#### Module 3A: Human Resilience and World War II — *Unbroken* & Internment (40 Lessons)
*Theme: How does the human spirit endure debilitating captivity and extreme adversity?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–13): From Delinquency to Olympic Glory & Internment**
  - Laura Hillenbrand: *Unbroken* (Louie Zamperini's youth & Pacific survival)
  - Miné Okubo: *Citizen 13660*
  - Franklin D. Roosevelt: *Executive Order 9066*
  - *Focus: Physical stamina, 47 days adrift in the Pacific, and Japanese-American wartime internment.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–19): POW Captivity & Unbroken Dignity**
  - Laura Hillenbrand: *Unbroken* (Omori, Naoetsu, and The Bird)
  - *Focus: Resilience against torture, holding the beam aloft, and psychological survival.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–8): Culminating Book Review & Thematic Reflection**
  - *Focus: Post-war trauma, redemption through forgiveness, and independent book review writing.*

#### Module 3B: Civil Rights & Desegregation — *A Mighty Long Way* & The Little Rock Nine (41 Lessons)
*Theme: How did courageous teenagers dismantle Jim Crow segregation in American schools?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–13): The Crisis at Central High**
  - Carlotta Walls LaNier: *A Mighty Long Way*
  - Dwight D. Eisenhower: *Address on the Situation in Little Rock* (1957)
  - *Focus: Signing up for history, facing the armed National Guard and screaming mob, and presidential intervention.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–20): Everyday Resistance & The Lost Year**
  - Carlotta Walls LaNier: *A Mighty Long Way*
  - *Focus: Surviving hostile hallways, the closing of Little Rock high schools, home bombing, and perseverance.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–8): Civil Rights Hero Informational Guide**
  - *Focus: May 1960 graduation, youth activism, and drafting informational brochures honoring unsung civil rights heroes.*

#### Module 4: Sustainability & Food Systems — *The Omnivore's Dilemma* (38 Lessons)
*Theme: Where does our food come from, and how should America feed itself sustainably?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–15): The Omnivore's Dilemma & Industrial Corn**
  - Michael Pollan: *The Omnivore's Dilemma*
  - *Focus: The modern supermarket dilemma, corn subsidies, and Concentrated Animal Feeding Operations (CAFOs).*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–17): Industrial Organic vs. Local Sustainable Farming**
  - Michael Pollan: *The Omnivore's Dilemma* (Polyface Farm & Joel Salatin)
  - *Focus: Closed-loop ecosystems, rotational grazing, Eggmobiles, and stakeholder consequence decision-making.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–6): Culminating Position Paper & Sustainability Poster**
  - *Focus: Synthesizing research evidence, taking a position on national food policy, and creating educational advocacy posters.*

### Universal Accessibility (UDL & WCAG AA/AAA)
- **Problem Set Interactivity**: Every lesson includes textareas with instant `localStorage` persistence, preventing any student progress loss.
- **Teacher Answer Keys**: Expandable reference solutions provide exemplary evidence citations and analysis rubrics.
- **Screen Reader Compatibility**: Semantic headings (`h1`–`h4`), ARIA live regions, and descriptive labels ensure seamless operation with JAWS, NVDA, and VoiceOver.
- **Multimodal Learning**: Dyslexia font options (OpenDyslexic), bionic reading, Irlen color tints, and high-contrast view modes are fully active across all lessons and reading pages.
