## Grade 11 Algebra II Curriculum & Master 120-Lesson Buildout

The Grade 11 Algebra II curriculum represents a rigorous, standards-aligned high school mathematics course encompassing 120 interactive lessons across four core instructional modules. Derived directly from authentic EngageNY / Eureka Math source materials, every lesson features deep polynomial arithmetic, rational and radical function modeling, periodic trigonometry with radian measure, exponential and logarithmic dynamics, inferential statistics with normal distributions, worked teacher examples with MathJax LaTeX typography, embedded official Problem Set worksheets with expandable teacher solution keys, and verbatim Exit Ticket assessments.

### Curriculum Structure & Module Inventory
- **Module 1: Polynomial, Rational, and Radical Relationships (Lessons 1–40)**:
  - *Topic A (Lessons 1–11)*: Polynomial arithmetic from base ten to base $x$, polynomial multiplication and division, long division and synthetic division, mental math strategies, Pythagorean triples, and factoring strategies.
  - *Topic B (Lessons 12–21)*: Overcoming factoring obstacles, graphing factored polynomial functions, end behavior and multiplicity of roots, polynomial modeling, the Remainder Theorem, and the Factor Theorem.
  - *Topic C (Lessons 22–35)*: Equivalent rational expressions, adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational expressions, solving rational equations, radical operations, linear systems in three variables, and the geometric definition of a parabola (focus and directrix).
  - *Topic D (Lessons 36–40)*: Complex numbers as solutions to equations, geometric representation in the complex plane, arithmetic of complex numbers ($a + bi$), complex conjugates, and the Fundamental Theorem of Algebra.
- **Module 2: Trigonometric Functions (Lessons 1–17)**:
  - *Topic A (Lessons 1–10)*: The story of trigonometry and real-world contexts, Ferris wheel height and co-height functions, motion of astronomical bodies, defining sine, cosine, and tangent on the unit circle, radian angle measure, and graphs of trigonometric functions.
  - *Topic B (Lessons 11–17)*: Transforming sinusoidal graphs (amplitude, frequency, period, midline, phase shift), modeling periodic phenomena (tides, sound waves, cyclical markets), the tangent function and its vertical asymptotes, and proving trigonometric identities (Pythagorean identity $\sin^2\theta + \cos^2\theta = 1$).
- **Module 3: Exponential and Logarithmic Functions (Lessons 1–33)**:
  - *Topic A (Lessons 1–6)*: Real numbers, integer and rational exponents, properties of exponents and radicals, irrational exponents, and Euler's number $e$.
  - *Topic B (Lessons 7–15)*: Exponential growth, defining logarithms as the inverse of exponential functions ('WhatPower'), common logarithms, logarithm properties (product, quotient, power rules), changing the base, and solving logarithmic equations.
  - *Topic C (Lessons 16–22)*: Rational and irrational numbers, graphing logarithmic and exponential functions, vertical and horizontal asymptotes, the natural logarithm function $\ln(x)$, and choosing mathematical models.
  - *Topic D (Lessons 23–28)*: Using logarithms in modeling situations, solving exponential equations using logs, geometric sequences, continuous compounding $A = P e^{rt}$, percent rate of change, and Newton's Law of Cooling.
  - *Topic E (Lessons 29–33)*: Culminating financial literacy project: geometric series formulas, structured savings plans, calculating auto loan and mortgage amortizations, credit card interest, and the million-dollar problem.
- **Module 4: Inferences and Conclusions from Data (Lessons 1–30)**:
  - *Topic A (Lessons 1–7)*: Chance experiments, sample spaces, calculating probabilities using two-way frequency tables, conditional probability $P(A|B)$, evaluating independence, Venn diagrams, and general probability rules.
  - *Topic B (Lessons 8–11)*: Modeling data distributions (shape, center, spread), smooth density curves, normal distributions, standard deviation, and the 68-95-99.7 Empirical Rule.
  - *Topic C (Lessons 12–22)*: Types of statistical studies (surveys, observational studies, experiments), sample statistics vs. population parameters, sampling variability in sample proportions and means, calculating margin of error, and evaluating published media reports.
  - *Topic D (Lessons 23–30)*: Designing statistical experiments, the critical role of random assignment, differences due to chance alone, permutation simulations, and drawing valid causal conclusions.

### Interactive Features & Study Tools
- **Embedded Problem Set Worksheets**: Complete practice problem sets with multi-part exercises directly embedded on every lesson page with zero external PDF download requirements.

- **Dynamic MathJax Typography**: All polynomial terms, trigonometric ratios, logarithmic expressions, radicals, and statistical symbols typeset cleanly into accessible vector SVGs.

### Keyboard Shortcuts & Action Controls
- `Tab` / `Shift+Tab`: Navigate systematically through lesson sections, formula cards, and interactive controls.
- `Enter` or `Space`: Expand/collapse solution keys and activate practice questions.
- `Alt + P`: Print the current lesson problem set worksheet.
- `Alt + B`: Bookmark the active lesson to your personal dashboard.
- `Esc`: Close open modal dialogues and overlays.
