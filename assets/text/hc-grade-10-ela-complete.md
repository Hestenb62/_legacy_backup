## Grade 10 English Language Arts (Level L): Full EngageNY Curriculum & Digital Library Integration

### Executive Overview
Grade 10 English Language Arts (Level L) provides a complete, rigorous, standards-aligned high school curriculum spanning 148 lessons across 4 core modules. Anchored in seminal works of world literature, Shakespearean tragedy, civil rights rhetoric, investigative journalism, bioethics research, and political philosophy, Level L equips sophomore students with advanced analytical reading, argumentative writing, and evidence-based synthesis skills.

All anchor literary selections are directly integrated with the Digital Library as standalone reading pages (`/library/?page=<slug>`), ensuring distraction-free reading without cluttering book catalog shelves.

---

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Reading Closely and Writing to Learn (34 Lessons)
*Theme: How Do Complex Characters Develop Through Their Interactions?*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–7): Pastoral Poetry Dialogue**
  - Christopher Marlowe: *"The Passionate Shepherd to His Love"*
  - Sir Walter Raleigh: *"The Nymph's Reply to the Shepherd"*
  - William Carlos Williams: *"Raleigh Was Right"*
  - *Focus: Analyzing pastoral conventions, speaker perspective, rhyme scheme, and modernist anti-pastoral critique.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 8–20): Short Story Analysis**
  - Ethan Canin: *"The Palace Thief"*
  - *Focus: Character development of Mr. Hundert and Sedgewick Bell, moral compromise, classical Roman virtue, and tragic irony.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 21–34): Literary Nonfiction & Culture**
  - H.G. Bissinger: *Friday Night Lights*
  - *Focus: Investigative journalism, community identity in Odessa, Texas, high school athletics, and societal pressure.*

#### Module 2: The Power of Persuasion: Rhetoric, Claims, and Human Rights (37 Lessons)
*Theme: Analyzing Rhetorical Strategies, Author Claims, and Foundational Civil Rights*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–20): Rhetoric & Civil Disobedience**
  - Martin Luther King, Jr.: *"Letter from Birmingham Jail"*
  - *Focus: Rhetorical appeals (logos, ethos, pathos), just vs. unjust laws, counterargument refutation, and urgency.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 21–30): Personal Narrative & Political Memory**
  - Julia Alvarez: *"A Genetics of Justice"*
  - *Focus: Impact of the Trujillo dictatorship on the author's family, the ethics of silence, and memory as justice.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 31–37): Foundational Human Rights Documents**
  - United Nations: *The Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (1948)
  - Eleanor Roosevelt: *"On the Adoption of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights"*
  - *Focus: Structural argumentation, foundational principles of human dignity, and international consensus.*

#### Module 3: Inquiry, Investigation, and Synthesis (39 Lessons)
*Theme: Researching Complex Bioethical Questions*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–15): Seed Text & Inquiry Generation**
  - Rebecca Skloot: *The Immortal Life of Henrietta Lacks*
  - *Focus: Informed consent, tissue ownership, racial disparities in medicine, and formulating inquiry paths.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 16–28): Source Evaluation & Synthesis**
  - *Focus: Gathering academic sources, assessing credibility, tracking claims and counterclaims in research portfolios.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 29–39): Evidence-Based Argument Writing**
  - *Focus: Drafting, revising, and finalizing multi-page academic research argument papers with formal citations.*

#### Module 4: Disorder, Tragedy, and Power (38 Lessons)
*Theme: Narrative Arc, Classical Drama, and Political Realism*
- **Topic A (Lessons 1–7): Tragic Arc in Personal Essay**
  - E.B. White: *"Death of a Pig"*
  - *Focus: Personal essay structure, tone modulation between farce and grief, and the classic tragic script.*
- **Topic B (Lessons 8–33): Dramatic Tragedy & Ambition**
  - William Shakespeare: *The Tragedy of Macbeth*
  - *Focus: Soliloquies, dramatic irony, motifs of blood and sleep, moral decay, and tragic downfall.*
- **Topic C (Lessons 34–38): Political Philosophy & Statecraft**
  - Niccolò Machiavelli: *The Prince*
  - *Focus: Practical realism vs. idealism, cruelty vs. clemency, and whether it is safer to be feared than loved.*

### Interactive Features & Tools
- **Digital Library Anchor Links:** Every lesson features a prominent "Read in Library" link opening the exact text selection in the Digital Library (`/library/?page=<slug>`).
- **Close Reading Question Sets:** Multi-step analytical questions targeting specific line numbers, diction, and character motivation.
- **Synthesizing Quick Writes:** End-of-lesson formal writing prompts evaluated against the Short Response Rubric.
- **Auto-Saving Textareas:** Student writing is immediately persisted in browser `localStorage`, ensuring work is never lost.
- **Expandable Teacher Keys:** Complete exemplar answers, textual evidence citations, and scoring rubrics.
- **1-Click Printing:** `printProblemSet()` produces clean, distraction-free worksheets for offline binders and classroom submissions.

---

### Accessibility & UDL Features
- **WCAG AA/AAA Compliance:** 100% keyboard navigable (`Tab`, `Shift+Tab`, `Enter`, `Space`, `Esc`).
- **High-Contrast Visibility:** Explicit `:focus-visible` focus rings and theme-adaptive colors across light, dark, midnight, and high-contrast modes.
- **Universal Design for Learning:** OpenDyslexic font support, adjustable text sizing, line spacing, Irlen color tints, and text-to-speech read-aloud capabilities.
- **Offline Resiliency:** Complete curriculum data and library pages function seamlessly offline through service worker caching.
