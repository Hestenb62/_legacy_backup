## Grade 5 English Language Arts (Level G): Full Expeditionary Learning / EngageNY Curriculum & Upper Elementary Launch

### Executive Overview
Grade 5 English Language Arts (Level G) marks the official launch of the Upper Elementary English curriculum on Hesten's Learning platform. Spanning 224 comprehensive, standards-aligned lessons across 6 full modules (Modules 1, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, and 4), Level G bridges foundational elementary literacy with rigorous textual analysis, primary source critique, and evidence-based synthesis.

Fifth graders explore fundamental human rights and Mexican-American labor history through Pam Muñoz Ryan's *Esperanza Rising* and the United Nations *Universal Declaration of Human Rights*, investigate rainforest canopy ecology through Kathryn Lasky's *The Most Beautiful Roof in the World* and Aaron Shepard's *The Wings of the Butterfly*, explore scientific inquiry and inventions with Donald B. Lemke's *Investigating the Scientific Method with Max Axiom*, examine racial justice and the courage of barrier-breaking athletes through Sharon Robinson's *Promises to Keep: Jackie Robinson*, study Indigenous engineering and survival technologies in Alootook Ipellie's *The Inuit Thought of It: Amazing Arctic Innovations*, and analyze the science of natural disasters and disaster relief through earthquake, hurricane, and American Red Cross case studies.

With the release of Grade 5, total platform curriculum lessons now stand at **3,236 lessons** in `assets/data/lessons.json`, supported by 74 dedicated Digital Library reading pages.

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Human Rights and Social Justice — *Esperanza Rising* (41 Lessons)
*Theme: What are human rights, how do people respond when their rights are threatened, and how are these struggles reflected in literature?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–11): Building Background Knowledge on Human Rights**
  - Pam Muñoz Ryan: *Esperanza Rising* (Chapters 1–4: Aguascalientes, Las Uvas, and family upheaval)
  - United Nations: *Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (Plain Language Youth Edition, Articles 1–15)
  - *Focus: Defining human rights, universal dignity, Mexican ranch life, and sudden social displacement.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–18): Analyzing Esperanza Rising Through Human Rights**
  - Pam Muñoz Ryan: *Esperanza Rising* (Chapters 5–10: Migration to California, labor camp conditions, strikes)
  - United Nations: *Universal Declaration of Human Rights* (Articles 16–30: Labor rights and equal protection)
  - *Focus: Analyzing character perspectives (Esperanza vs. Marta), labor camp conditions, and connecting fictional events to specific UDHR articles.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–12): Readers Theater & Human Rights Monologues**
  - *Focus: Writing and performing original monologues and readers theater scripts capturing characters facing human rights dilemmas.*

#### Module 2A: Biodiversity in the Rainforest — *The Most Beautiful Roof in the World* (40 Lessons)
*Theme: What is biodiversity, how do scientists study rainforest ecosystems, and how do organisms adapt to their environment?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): Biodiversity in the Rainforest Canopy**
  - Kathryn Lasky & Meg Lowman: *The Most Beautiful Roof in the World*
  - *Focus: Meg Lowman's pioneering ascent into the canopy, epiphytes, bromeliads, and vertical forest strata.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–15): Interdependence of Species and Indigenous Folklore**
  - Kathryn Lasky: *The Most Beautiful Roof in the World* (Herbivory, leaf cutter ants, and canopy food webs)
  - Aaron Shepard: *The Wings of the Butterfly: An Amazon Legend*
  - *Focus: Comparing scientific text structure with indigenous Amazonian folklore, plant-animal symbiosis, and moral themes.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–15): Rainforest Field Journal and Conservation Research**
  - *Focus: Crafting a multi-entry scientist field journal documenting Amazon canopy biodiversity and rainforest preservation arguments.*

#### Module 2B: Investigating the Scientific Method & Inventions — *Max Axiom* (40 Lessons)
*Theme: How do scientists use empirical inquiry to solve problems, and how do technological inventions transform society?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): The Scientific Method & Problem Solving**
  - Donald B. Lemke: *Investigating the Scientific Method with Max Axiom, Super Scientist*
  - *Focus: Formulating testable questions, reading graphic non-fiction science texts, and identifying independent and dependent variables.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–13): Developing Hypotheses and Controlled Experiments**
  - *Focus: Designing controlled trials, recording quantitative and qualitative data, and evaluating experimental validity.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–17): Evidence-Based Science Lab Report & Innovation Showcase**
  - *Focus: Writing comprehensive lab reports with data tables, graphs, and evidence-based scientific conclusions on transformative inventions.*

#### Module 3A: Athletes Breaking Barriers — *Promises to Keep: Jackie Robinson* (39 Lessons)
*Theme: How do athletic pioneers break social barriers, and what character traits are required to challenge racial prejudice?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–7): Jackie Robinson and the Civil Rights Movement**
  - Sharon Robinson: *Promises to Keep: How Jackie Robinson Changed America* (Chapters 1–2)
  - *Focus: Jim Crow segregation, the Robinson family values, Negro Leagues baseball, and early civil rights challenges.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–17): Breaking the Color Barrier in Major League Baseball**
  - Sharon Robinson: *Promises to Keep* (Chapters 3–5: Branch Rickey, the noble experiment, 1947 rookie season)
  - Biographical Studies: *Roberto Clemente & Althea Gibson*
  - *Focus: Branch Rickey's vision, nonviolent courage on the baseball diamond, and overcoming prejudice in tennis and baseball.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–15): Opinion Essay: The Legacy of Athletic Pioneers**
  - *Focus: Drafting, revising, and presenting an evidence-based opinion essay evaluating the lasting social impact of barrier-breaking athletes.*

#### Module 3B: Arctic Innovations & Culture — *The Inuit Thought of It* (31 Lessons)
*Theme: How have Indigenous peoples engineered innovative survival solutions in extreme environments, and how is cultural knowledge preserved?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–9): Arctic Environment and Survival Technologies**
  - Alootook Ipellie with David MacDonald: *The Inuit Thought of It: Amazing Arctic Innovations* (Chapters 1–3)
  - *Focus: Extreme Arctic geography, thermal physics of igluvijaq (snowhouses), and multi-layered animal skin clothing engineering.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–10): Transportation and Hunting Innovations**
  - Alootook Ipellie: *The Inuit Thought of It* (Chapters 4–6)
  - *Focus: Engineering of dog sleds (qamutiik), sea kayaks (qajaq), large family boats (umiak), toggling harpoons, and snow goggles.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–12): Community Storytelling & Culminating Museum Exhibition**
  - *Focus: Curating a virtual Arctic innovation exhibition with detailed informational placards explaining Inuit technology and cultural resilience.*

#### Module 4: Science and Society — Natural Disasters & Humanitarian Aid (33 Lessons)
*Theme: What natural processes cause extreme weather and seismic events, and how do communities and aid organizations respond to crises?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–8): The Science of Natural Disasters: Hurricanes & Cyclones**
  - Science Research: *Atmospheric Mechanics of Hurricanes, Storm Surges, and Satellite Meteorology*
  - *Focus: How warm ocean waters fuel tropical cyclones, atmospheric pressure systems, and interpreting weather maps and charts.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–9): Earthquakes & Emergency Humanitarian Relief**
  - Case Studies: *Tectonic Plate Boundaries, Seismic Waves, and the 2010 Haiti Earthquake*
  - American Red Cross: *Mobilizing Disaster Logistics, Search and Rescue, and Clean Water Distribution*
  - *Focus: Cause-and-effect text structures, disaster timeline analysis, and evaluating humanitarian aid logistics.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–16): Public Service Announcements & Disaster Preparedness**
  - *Focus: Writing informative public service announcements (PSAs) and personal emergency response plans citing earth science facts.*

### Core Interactive Features & Shortcuts
- **Direct Level & Subject Access**:
  - Grade 5 Hub: `/levels/g.php`
  - Grade 5 ELA Direct: `/levels/g.php?subject=ela`
  - Direct Lesson URLs: `/levels/g.php?g-ela-m1-u1-l1`
- **Embedded Problem Sets**:
  - Close-reading text-dependent questions and quick writes embedded directly into every lesson.
  - Auto-saving student responses stored locally in browser `localStorage`.
  - Expandable teacher answer keys with model exemplary responses and scaffolding tips.
  - 1-Click clean browser printing via `printProblemSet()`.
- **Keyboard Navigation**:
  - `Tab` / `Shift+Tab`: Navigate interactive controls, textareas, and tabs.
  - `Enter` / `Space`: Toggle answer keys, accordions, and action buttons.
  - `Esc`: Close modals and overlay dialogues.

### Accessibility & Universal Design for Learning (UDL) Compliance
- **Perceivable**: Strict WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA (≥4.5:1) and AAA (≥7:1) color contrast in light, dark, and high-contrast modes.
- **Multimodal Representation**: Digital Library reading pages provide clean semantic typography, OpenDyslexic font toggles, Irlen color overlays, and synthesized text-to-speech read-aloud capabilities.
- **Flexible Expression**: Students can respond via keyboard input, speech dictation, or printed problem set worksheets.
- **Low-Anxiety & Untimed Accommodations**: Untimed practice mode and auto-save eliminate time pressure and prevent accidental data loss.
- **Offline Resilient**: Fully operational through service worker caching via `offline.php`.
