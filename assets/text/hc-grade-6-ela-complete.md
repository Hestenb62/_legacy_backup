## Grade 6 English Language Arts (Level H): Full Expeditionary Learning / EngageNY Curriculum & Middle School Completion

### Executive Overview
Grade 6 English Language Arts (Level H) serves as the foundational cornerstone of the Middle School English curriculum on Hesten's Learning platform. Spanning 227 comprehensive lessons across 6 full modules (Modules 1, 2A, 2B, 3A, 3B, and 4), Level H immerses sixth graders in Greek mythology, archetypal hero narratives, Depression-era resilience, medieval social roles, cross-cultural immigrant perspectives, ocean conservation science, and ecological decision-making.

With the completion of Grade 6, **Middle School English Language Arts (Grades 6–8, Levels H, I, J) is 100% complete across all 18 modules, totaling 704 middle school lessons** and 37 dedicated Digital Library reading pages. Across the entire platform, verified curriculum lessons now officially exceed 3,000 to reach **3,012 total lessons**.

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Myth, Ritual, and The Hero's Journey — *The Lightning Thief* (40 Lessons)
*Theme: What is the hero's journey, what makes a myth, and why do ancient myths continue to matter?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–13): Percy Jackson and Greek Mythology**
  - Rick Riordan: *The Lightning Thief* (Chapters 1–8: Boarding school challenges, Mrs. Dodds, Camp Half-Blood)
  - Joseph Campbell: *"The Hero's Journey: The Monomyth Archetype"*
  - *Focus: Percy's point of view, character identity, ADHD/dyslexia as battle instincts, and Campbell's Departure stage.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–20): Elements of Myth & The Road of Trials**
  - Rick Riordan: *The Lightning Thief* (Chapters 9–22: Medusa, Lotus Casino, Underworld, Ares)
  - *Focus: Analyzing mythological allusions (Cronus, Medusa, Prometheus), Initiation stage, Supreme Ordeal, and thematic synthesis.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–7): Hero's Journey Narrative Writing**
  - *Focus: Writing an original myth narrative that follows the hero's journey archetype with vivid sensory details and dialogue.*

#### Module 2A: Rules to Live By — *Bud, Not Buddy* (38 Lessons)
*Theme: How do people formulate rules to live by to survive and improve their lives during hardship?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–12): Launching Bud, Not Buddy & Personal Rules**
  - Christopher Paul Curtis: *Bud, Not Buddy* (Chapters 1–7)
  - Steve Jobs: *Stanford University Commencement Address* (2005)
  - Rudyard Kipling: *"If—"*
  - *Focus: Bud's Rules and Things, humor as a survival mechanism, figurative language, and comparing philosophies of life.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–17): The Great Depression & Hoovervilles**
  - Christopher Paul Curtis: *Bud, Not Buddy* (Chapters 8–19: Hoovervilles, Lefty Lewis, Herman E. Calloway)
  - *Focus: Historical context of the Depression, jazz culture, labor organizing, character development, and point of view.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–9): Argumentative Essay: Rules to Live By**
  - *Focus: Writing an evidence-based argument essay analyzing how Bud used his rules to thrive and survive adversity.*

#### Module 2B: Voices of Adversity — *Good Masters! Sweet Ladies!* (39 Lessons)
*Theme: How did young people in medieval society navigate adversity, rigid social hierarchies, and self-expression?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–13): Feudal Society & Medieval Roles**
  - Laura Amy Schlitz: *Good Masters! Sweet Ladies! Voices from a Medieval Village*
  - *Focus: The lord of the manor, peasants, blacksmiths, shepherds, and close reading monologues in verse and prose.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–16): Analyzing Voices from a Medieval Village**
  - *Focus: Comparing noble and peasant perspectives (Hugo vs. Taggot, Will vs. Alice), poetic meter, and adversity across social classes.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–10): Performance Task: Writing and Performing Medieval Monologues**
  - *Focus: Researching medieval trades and crafting original first-person monologues to be performed in readers theater style.*

#### Module 3A: Point of View and Cultural Perspective — *Dragonwings* (36 Lessons)
*Theme: How does an author's cultural perspective shape point of view and narrative voice?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): Moon Shadow and the Tang People**
  - Laurence Yep: *Dragonwings* (Chapters 1–5) & *The Lost Garden: A Memoir*
  - *Focus: Moon Shadow's arrival in San Francisco, Chinese immigrant culture, the "Land of the Golden Mountain," and the demon world.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–14): The Flight of Dragonwings & The 1906 Earthquake**
  - Laurence Yep: *Dragonwings* (Chapters 6–12)
  - *Focus: Father Windrider's dream of flight, Miss Whitlaw's friendship, surviving the 1906 earthquake, and the soaring of Dragonwings.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–12): Research-Based Earthquake Narrative**
  - *Focus: Integrating historical research on the 1906 San Francisco earthquake into a rich, creative narrative from an immigrant's perspective.*

#### Module 3B: Point of View and Marine Conservation — *World Without Fish* (36 Lessons)
*Theme: How do authors convey point of view and craft persuasive arguments about environmental crises?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–11): Cod Collapse & The World's Oceans**
  - Mark Kurlansky: *World Without Fish* (Chapters 1–4)
  - *Focus: The Grand Banks fishery collapse, technological advancements in trawling, and analyzing an author's developing argument.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–12): Marine Food Webs & Ocean Ecology**
  - Mark Kurlansky: *World Without Fish* (Chapters 5–8)
  - *Focus: Interdependent marine food webs, ocean acidification, marine protected areas, and author's craft.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–13): Informational Consumer Guide: Sustainable Seafood**
  - *Focus: Synthesizing ecological evidence to create a graphic informational brochure guiding consumers toward sustainable seafood choices.*

#### Module 4: Decision Making & The Environment — *Frightful's Mountain* (38 Lessons)
*Theme: How do human activities impact ecosystems, and how do we balance benefits with environmental consequences?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–13): Frightful's Perspective: Falcon Biology & Rachel Carson**
  - Jean Craighead George: *Frightful's Mountain* (Chapters 1–7)
  - Rachel Carson & Robert Peterson: *Silent Spring & Sounding the Alarm on Pollution*
  - *Focus: Third-person limited point of view through falcon eyes, peregrine biology, imprinting, and the history of DDT.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–15): Research Study: Benefits vs. Harm of DDT**
  - *Focus: Investigating the DDT controversy: malaria eradication vs. raptor eggshell thinning, bioaccumulation, and Cascading Consequences charts.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–10): Evidence-Based Position Paper on DDT**
  - *Focus: Writing a comprehensive position paper taking a researched stance on pesticide use and presenting findings in a symposium format.*
