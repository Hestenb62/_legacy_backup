## Grade 7 English Language Arts (Level I): Full Expeditionary Learning / EngageNY Curriculum

### Executive Overview
Grade 7 English Language Arts (Level I) delivers a comprehensive, inquiry-driven middle school curriculum on Hesten's Learning platform. Spanning 238 rigorous lessons across 6 modules (Modules 1, 2A, 2B, 3, 4A, and 4B), Level I empowers 7th-grade students to analyze dual character perspectives, investigate historical and modern working conditions, evaluate social stereotypes, examine the rhetoric of abolition and freedom, and grapple with real-world scientific dilemmas surrounding adolescent neurodevelopment and global water sustainability.

With the completion of Grade 7, the platform's verified curriculum expands to **2,785 total lessons** across Levels A through N, supported by 11 dedicated Digital Library reading pages.

---

### Core Curriculum Modules

#### Module 1: Journeys and Survival — *A Long Walk to Water* (39 Lessons)
*Theme: How do individuals survive in challenging environments, and how do dual perspectives reveal character?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–14): Coming of Age & The Second Sudanese Civil War**
  - Linda Sue Park: *A Long Walk to Water* (Chapters 1–5: Salva Dut & Nya)
  - Historical Archives / UNHCR: *"The Second Sudanese Civil War and the Lost Boys"*
  - *Focus: Dual narrative points of view, Dinka and Nuer tribal cultures, fleeing village conflict, and establishing evidence-based note-catchers.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–19): Survival in Challenging Environments**
  - Linda Sue Park: *A Long Walk to Water* (Chapters 6–18: Akobo Desert, Gilo River, refugee camps)
  - *Focus: Physical and psychological survival factors, environmental challenges, waterborne illnesses, and drafting an evidence-based literary analysis essay.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–6): Culminating Two-Voice Poetry Performance Task**
  - *Focus: Synthesizing character analysis and historical research to write and perform a two-voice poem contrasting Salva and Nya's experiences.*

#### Module 2A: Working Conditions — *Lyddie* & The Labor Movement (38 Lessons)
*Theme: What are working conditions, and what role do workers, businesses, and consumers play in improving them?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–20): Working Conditions in Industrializing America**
  - Katherine Paterson: *Lyddie* (Chapters 1–14: Vermont farm debt, Cutler's Tavern, Lowell textile mills)
  - *Focus: Analyzing how plot, character, and setting interact in industrializing New England, loom room hazards, and writing an argumentative essay on whether Lyddie should sign the ten-hour petition.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–8): The Labor Movement & Farmworker Empowerment**
  - César Chávez: *Commonwealth Club Address* (1984)
  - *Focus: Analyzing rhetorical arguments, the United Farm Workers movement, nonviolent consumer boycotts, and civil rights.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–10): Researching the Modern Garment Industry**
  - *Focus: Conducting research on contemporary sweatshops and corporate supply chains, culminating in a teen consumer's guide to ethical garment shopping.*

#### Module 2B: Personal Identity & Transformation — *Pygmalion* (42 Lessons)
*Theme: How does society try to define individuals, and how do individuals define themselves?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): Exploring Identity and Social Identifiers**
  - George Bernard Shaw: *Pygmalion* (Act 1: Covent Garden and Social Dialects)
  - *Focus: Exploring race, gender, and class identifiers, analyzing how authors develop claims about personal identity, and evaluating social status.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–19): Eliza Doolittle's Transformation**
  - George Bernard Shaw: *Pygmalion* (Acts 2–4: Higgins's Laboratory & The Ambassador's Ball)
  - *Focus: Analyzing character development, internal vs. external transformation, class mobility, and writing an argument essay on whether Eliza achieved true independence.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–13): Media, Gender Roles, and Counter-Advertising**
  - *Focus: Investigating print media advertising, analyzing gender stereotypes, and creating a creative "counter-ad" that challenges conventional gender norms.*

#### Module 3: What Gives Stories Power? — *Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass* (43 Lessons)
*Theme: How can language, imagery, and personal testimony be used to confront injustice and assert human dignity?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–15): The Power of Storytelling & Abolitionist Poetry**
  - Frederick Douglass: *Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass* (Chapters 1–7)
  - Philip Freneau: *"The African Chief"* & Paul Laurence Dunbar: *"We Wear the Mask"*
  - *Focus: Figurative language, sound repetition, childhood on plantation estates, Baltimore arrival, and literacy as the pathway to freedom.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–16): Close Reading the Slave Narrative**
  - Frederick Douglass: *Narrative of the Life of Frederick Douglass* (Chapters 8–11: Slave breaker Covey)
  - *Focus: Analyzing authorial purpose and audience, the physical and psychological struggle for manhood, and escape to New York.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–12): Illustrated Children's Biography Performance Task**
  - *Focus: Adapting an episode from Douglass's autobiography into an illustrated children's picture book with accessible vocabulary and author's note.*

#### Module 4A: Science and Society — Adolescent Brain Development & Screen Time (39 Lessons)
*Theme: How does digital entertainment media affect the neurological development of adolescents?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): The Adolescent Brain Under Construction**
  - Dr. Jay Giedd & NIDA: *"Teens and Decision Making: What Brain Science Reveals"*
  - *Focus: Prefrontal cortex development, the limbic system, synaptic pruning, and neuroplasticity ("use it or lose it").*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–19): Research Study: Screen Time and Cognitive Focus**
  - Matt Richtel: *"Growing Up Digital"* & NPR: *"The Teen Brain"*
  - *Focus: Analyzing research studies on digital multitasking, video game stimulation, sleep deprivation, and social media dopamine loops.*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–10): Position Paper and Visual Presentation**
  - *Focus: Synthesizing scientific evidence to write an argument position paper recommending evidence-based screen-time guidelines for schools and families.*

#### Module 4B: Science and Society — Water Sustainability & Freshwater Management (37 Lessons)
*Theme: How can society balance human agricultural/industrial needs with freshwater ecological sustainability?*
- **Unit 1 (Lessons 1–10): Water Sustainability: The Finite Cycle of Life**
  - Barbara Kingsolver: *"Water Is Life"* (National Geographic, 2010)
  - *Focus: Global water scarcity, the 1% accessible freshwater commons, aquifer depletion, and tracing authorial arguments.*
- **Unit 2 (Lessons 1–17): Industrial and Agricultural Water Management**
  - Charles Fishman: *The Big Thirst* (2011)
  - *Focus: The end of "easy water," irrigation technologies, wastewater purification, and Stakeholder Consequences Decision-Making (SCDM).*
- **Unit 3 (Lessons 1–10): Culminating Water Policy Position Paper**
  - *Focus: Formulating an evidence-based position on national or regional water conservation policy, supported by an advocacy infographic display.*

### Universal Accessibility (UDL & WCAG AA/AAA)
- **Problem Set Interactivity**: Every lesson includes interactive textareas with instant `localStorage` persistence, preventing student progress loss.
- **Teacher Answer Keys**: Expandable reference solutions provide exemplary evidence citations and analysis rubrics.
- **Screen Reader Compatibility**: Semantic headings (`h1`–`h4`), ARIA live regions, and descriptive labels ensure seamless operation with JAWS, NVDA, and VoiceOver.
- **Multimodal Learning**: Dyslexia font options (OpenDyslexic), bionic reading, Irlen color tints, and high-contrast view modes are fully active across all lessons and reading pages.
