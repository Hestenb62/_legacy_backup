# Universal Design for Learning (UDL) Guidelines Version 2.2
**Authoritative Framework**: Center for Applied Special Technology (CAST)  
**Permanent URI**: `https://udlguidelines.cast.org/`  
**Foundational Research**: Educational Neuroscience, Cognitive Science & Special Education (Rose & Meyer, 2002; CAST, 2018)

---

## The UDL Conceptual Framework
Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a framework to guide the design of learning environments that are accessible, challenging, and engaging for all learners. Based on research on how humans learn, UDL addresses the primary barrier to learning: inflexible, one-size-fits-all curricula that create unintentional obstacles to learning.

UDL organizes learning interventions around three primary neurological brain networks:
1. **Affective Networks** (The "Why" of Learning): Motivation, interest, and engagement.
2. **Recognition Networks** (The "What" of Learning): How we gather facts and categorize what we see, hear, and read.
3. **Strategic Networks** (The "How" of Learning): Planning, executing, and monitoring actions and skills.

---

## Principle I: Provide Multiple Means of Engagement (Affective Networks)
To develop purposeful, motivated learners who are engaged in their education.

### Guideline 7: Provide options for Recruiting Interest
* **Checkpoint 7.1 Optimize individual choice and autonomy**: Offer choices in context, tools used for information gathering or production, color design, and learning sequence.
* **Checkpoint 7.2 Optimize relevance, value, and authenticity**: Design learning activities that match students' lived experiences, culturally relevant examples, and real-world utility.
* **Checkpoint 7.3 Minimize threats and distractions**: Reduce unnecessary visual noise, sensory stimulation, background distractions, and cognitive anxiety (e.g., Focus Mode, Reading Mask, Hide Images).

### Guideline 8: Provide options for Sustaining Effort & Persistence
* **Checkpoint 8.1 Heighten salience of goals and objectives**: Explicitly state objectives, standards benchmarks, and target mastery levels before lessons begin.
* **Checkpoint 8.2 Vary demands and resources to optimize challenge**: Differentiate difficulty levels, provide scaffolded practice drills, and allow personalized pacing.
* **Checkpoint 8.3 Foster collaboration and community**: Encourage cooperative peer learning and group problem solving.
* **Checkpoint 8.4 Increase mastery-oriented feedback**: Provide immediate, constructive formative feedback focusing on effort, progress, and skill development rather than comparative ranking.

### Guideline 9: Provide options for Self-Regulation
* **Checkpoint 9.1 Promote expectations and beliefs that optimize motivation**: Frame learning around growth mindsets, gamified daily streaks, and achievable milestones.
* **Checkpoint 9.2 Facilitate personal coping skills and strategies**: Provide built-in study timers, structured rest breaks, and emotional self-monitoring prompts.
* **Checkpoint 9.3 Develop self-assessment and reflection**: Offer student self-check tools, personal mastery dashboards, and progress tracking.

## Principle II: Provide Multiple Means of Representation (Recognition Networks)
To develop resourceful, knowledgeable learners who perceive and comprehend instructional content.

### Guideline 1: Provide options for Perception
* **Checkpoint 1.1 Offer ways of customizing the display of information**: Allow users to adjust font size, contrast, line height, letter spacing, word spacing, color tint overlays, and font family (e.g., OpenDyslexic, Lexend).
* **Checkpoint 1.2 Offer alternatives for auditory information**: Provide visual captions, transcripts, diagrams, and written summaries alongside spoken audio.
* **Checkpoint 1.3 Offer alternatives for visual information**: Provide synchronized Text-to-Speech (TTS), audio narration, descriptive labels, and tactile/printable formats.

### Guideline 2: Provide options for Language & Symbols
* **Checkpoint 2.1 Clarify vocabulary and symbols**: Provide on-demand glossary tooltips, embedded dictionary definitions, and visual symbol scaffolds.
* **Checkpoint 2.2 Clarify syntax and structure**: Highlight grammatical connections, sentence diagrams, and mathematical step-by-step logic.
* **Checkpoint 2.3 Support decoding of text, mathematical notation, and symbols**: Use phonetic pronunciation aids, Bionic Reading eye saccade fixations, and MathJax dynamic rendering.
* **Checkpoint 2.4 Promote understanding across languages**: Support multilingual translation overlays and culturally inclusive explanations.
* **Checkpoint 2.5 Illustrate through multiple media**: Present key concepts using text, illustrations, graphs, manipulatives, audio, and video simultaneously.

### Guideline 3: Provide options for Comprehension
* **Checkpoint 3.1 Activate or supply background knowledge**: Anchor new concepts to prior learning with interactive reviews, prerequisite skill maps, and concept hooks.
* **Checkpoint 3.2 Highlight patterns, critical features, big ideas, and relationships**: Use bold typographical emphasis, color cues, callout boxes, and summary diagrams to emphasize essential takeaways.
* **Checkpoint 3.3 Guide information processing and visualization**: Break complex multi-step problems into scaffolded chunks with interactive hints and progressive disclosure.
* **Checkpoint 3.4 Maximize transfer and generalization**: Provide opportunities to apply learned skills in varied problem contexts, worksheets, and practical tasks.

## Principle III: Provide Multiple Means of Action & Expression (Strategic Networks)
To develop strategic, goal-directed learners who can navigate and express their learning effectively.

### Guideline 4: Provide options for Physical Action
* **Checkpoint 4.1 Vary the methods for response and navigation**: Ensure all lessons and assessments are operable via keyboard alone, mouse, touch gesture, switch device, or voice input.
* **Checkpoint 4.2 Optimize access to tools and assistive technologies**: Support screen readers, speech-to-text, on-screen calculators, and scratchpads without software conflicts.

### Guideline 5: Provide options for Expression & Communication
* **Checkpoint 5.1 Use multiple media for communication**: Allow students to demonstrate mastery through multiple choice, typed answers, drawn work, or verbal recordings.
* **Checkpoint 5.2 Use multiple tools for construction and composition**: Provide digital scratchpads, virtual manipulatives, graph paper, and spell-check assistance.
* **Checkpoint 5.3 Build fluencies with graduated levels of support for practice and performance**: Provide tiered fluency sprints, diagnostic quizzes, and customizable difficulty ramps.

### Guideline 6: Provide options for Executive Functions
* **Checkpoint 6.1 Guide appropriate goal-setting**: Help students set achievable daily learning targets (e.g., 20-minute daily study goals).
* **Checkpoint 6.2 Support planning and strategy development**: Provide checklists, structured curriculum roadmaps, and 36-week pacing guides.
* **Checkpoint 6.3 Facilitate managing information and resources**: Offer scratchpads, bookmarking systems, and citation generators for academic synthesis.
* **Checkpoint 6.4 Enhance capacity for monitoring progress**: Provide visual progress bars, mastery velocity metrics, and transparent competency rubrics.
